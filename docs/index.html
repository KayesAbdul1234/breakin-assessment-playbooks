How to prepare

What to do in the days before, and how to set yourself up on the day.

Before the real thing

  • Try Arctic Shores' own free demo first. It takes about 5 minutes, with no sign-up.
  • Learn what every game is testing. Each card above tells you.
  • Play each game in practice mode until the controls feel natural, then do one full-length run.
  • Practise numerical, logical and abstract reasoning with free online tests for the cognitive games.
  • Do the official practice task in your invite. It doesn't count towards your score.
  • Need adjustments, such as extra time? Ask the employer before you start. They can't be added once you begin.

Things people get wrong

  • On Security, get as far as you can, then leave once you're stuck. Don't spend 40 minutes on one game.
  • The money, points and coins aren't your score. Don't chase them.
  • On the behavioural games, pick an approach and stay consistent. There's nothing to fake.
  • Use the Q and P keys on a laptop. Clicking the on-screen icons can make some tasks stop responding.

On the day

  • A device no more than about 4 years old, charged or plugged in
  • Stable WiFi, VPN and ad blockers off, every notification off
  • A quiet hour, so you can do it in one sitting
  • Your invite link, login and deadline ready

Before each game

  • Read the instructions properly
  • Use the tutorial and practice round it gives you
  • Settle your approach before the scored rounds start
  • Take a break between games if you need one, never during

One shot. Retakes usually aren't allowed in the same hiring round, and results can be reused by the same employer for up to a year. Check your invitation.

How to get better, step by step

Do this for each game your employer uses. If you don't know which ones, work through all 14.

  1. Learn it

    Open the game and read How to play and the example. It takes about two minutes.

  2. Practise it

    Play practice mode with hints until you know the rules and controls without thinking.

  3. Play full length

    Play the real number of rounds at the real speed, and aim for the target shown on the game.

  4. Fix your weakest

    Read your report after each run: what went well, what to work on, and why any answers were wrong. Then replay the games that aren't at target yet.

What mentees usually ask

How close are these to the real games?
Each game is built from what candidates and prep guides report: the same rules, controls, number of rounds and timing. Arctic Shores doesn't publish its tasks, so the look is ours and some details may differ. These are practice versions, not the real test, and there are no answers to memorise.
What do the targets mean?
They're BreakIn practice goals that show you're ready, not Arctic Shores pass marks. The real assessment has no pass mark: your results are compared with people who do well in the role. On the behavioural games there's no right answer, so the target is about learning how the game works and staying consistent.
Where is my progress saved?
In this browser, on this device only. Nobody else can see it. Use the same device and browser to keep your progress, and note that clearing your browser data resets it.
How many games will I get?
Up to nine, chosen by the employer for the role. Each takes about 5 to 15 minutes. Analysis and Patterns don't always appear. Arctic Shores also adds newer tasks, such as one on learning agility, so you may meet one that isn't covered here. If you do, the same approach works: read the instructions, use the practice round, and stay steady.
How do I read my Arctic Shores feedback?
If the employer turns feedback on, you get a report after the assessment. It places you on a 1 to 10 scale for each behaviour, compared with a large group of other candidates. Neither end of a scale is better or worse: a 2 and a 9 just describe different working styles. Bring your report to a BreakIn session and we'll go through it with you.
Can I prepare for the behavioural games?
You can learn how they work, but there's no better or worse answer. They're reading the real you, and acting a part shows up as inconsistency. Pick an approach and stick with it.
Do the money and points matter?
No. They aren't your score, so don't chase them. Your results come from how you play across the whole game.
What if a game keeps getting harder and I get stuck?
Get as far as you can, then stop. You can leave Security at any point, and spending 40 minutes on one game costs you more than it gains.
Can I retake it if it goes badly?
Usually not within the same hiring round, though it varies by employer. Check your invitation before you start.
